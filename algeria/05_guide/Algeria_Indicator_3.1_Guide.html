GBF Headline Indicator 3.1 · National worked example with real data

Algeria, step by step through the 30×30 tool

This guide fills the Indicator 3.1 Excel tool for Algeria from the 78 records in the October 2026 World Database on Protected Areas and World Database on OECMs, the 65 official Key Biodiversity Area polygons (September 2026), and a GIS run over land and sea. Every number comes from those data and from the workbook recalculated in Excel.

Algeria is a case where the OECM column decides the answer. Its protected areas cover under 5% of the land. Its five Saharan Cultural Parks, reported to the WD-OECM, cover almost half of it. Together they put the land figure far above 30%, while the sea stays below 0.1%.

STEP 01

Collect the data

Everything used here is in the algeria/01_data folder, with the source of each file:

  1. Protected areas and OECMs. WDPA and WD-OECM, October 2026, from the UNEP-WCMC map service behind Protected Planet: 71 protected-area polygons, 2 points and 5 OECM polygons, as shapefile, GeoJSON and GeoPackage.
  2. Base map. Land: the geoBoundaries ADM0 outline. Sea: the Marine Regions EEZ v12, Algeria's 200-nautical-mile zone, with coastal gaps closed (47 km²). Both projected to Mollweide.
  3. Key Biodiversity Areas. The World Database of KBAs, September 2026: 65 Algerian polygons and 2 points, plus 213 trigger-species records. The KBA data are licensed; don't republish the polygons.
  4. Management effectiveness. GD-PAME evaluations listed on the 78 site pages on Protected Planet: 16 sites carry at least one.
  5. Ecosystems, mountains and inland waters. RESOLVE Ecoregions 2017, Marine Ecoregions of the World, the GMBA Mountain Inventory v2.0, HydroRIVERS v1.0 and GLWD v2, clipped to Algeria by 00_prepare_layers.py.

STEP 03Outside Excel: 03_analysis/scripts/01_gis_flatten.py

Flatten PAs and OECMs in GIS

The metadata treats the two layers differently. Protected areas are dissolved into one flat layer. OECMs are then measured only where no protected area lies, so the same ground is never counted twice, and never as an OECM when it is already protected.

The procedure (metadata steps 1–6)

  1. Filter. All eight UNESCO biosphere reserves drop out (Tassili, El Kala, Djurdjura, Chréa, Taza, Gouraya, Belezma, Tlemcen). Their core zones are counted through the national parks and the World Heritage site.
  2. Buffer points. Two designated points become circles: Djebel Aïssa National Park (244 km²) and the Habibas islands marine reserve (27 km², all at sea).
  3. Clip and project. Each site is split into land and sea parts and measured in Mollweide.
  4. Dissolve PAs. 108,450 km² summed becomes 107,764 km² on land; at sea, 107 km² becomes 89.8 km².
  5. Erase PAs from OECMs, then dissolve. The Cultural Parks sum to 1,233,094 km². Removing the protected areas inside them, above all the 75,470 km² of the Tassili n'Ajjer World Heritage Site inside the Tassili Cultural Park, leaves 1,151,696 km².
  6. Attribute overlaps. Protected areas first, then OECMs; within each, the earliest designation keeps the overlap. Three Ramsar records lose all their area: Lac Oubeïra (inside El Kala National Park), Vallée d'Iherir (inside the Tassili World Heritage site) and Tourbière du Lac Noir.
STEP 04Sheet: PA_OECM_Register

Enter the site register

All 78 records go in. Column E says whether each is a protected area or an OECM; the tool keeps the two apart in every table. Paste the attributes into the yellow columns and the net land and sea areas into R and S.

The Tassili Cultural Park keeps 63,128 of its 138,579 km² as OECM, because the World Heritage site inside it is counted as a protected area. The two Habibas records show the point rule at sea: the 2003 reserve circle holds 26.8 km², the 2005 SPAMI polygon only the 9.2 km² outside it.

STEP 05Sheet: Headline_3.1

Read the headline

Result. PAs and OECMs cover 54.32% of Algeria's land, so the land element of Target 3 is met. They cover 0.07% of its waters: 90 km² out of 131,440 km². Reaching 30% at sea would take another 39,342 km²; even the national target of 5% needs another 6,482 km².
STEP 06

The OECM question

The land result rests on one decision: whether the five Cultural Parks are OECMs. Algeria has reported them to the WD-OECM, and Protected Planet counts them, so the indicator does too. Without them, coverage would be 4.65%.

What to check before reporting

An OECM must meet the criteria in CBD decision 14/8: a defined area, governed and managed in ways that achieve positive, long-term outcomes for biodiversity, whatever its primary purpose. The Cultural Parks protect cultural and natural heritage together and cover mostly desert. Your report should state the basis for their OECM status, and the management-effectiveness step below shows how thin the evidence on their outcomes is: one METT assessment each, from 2011.

Report the PA and OECM figures side by side, as the tool does (4.65% and 49.67%), rather than the total alone.

STEP 07Sheet: Ecosystems

Ecosystems and inland waters

The flat layers were intersected with Algeria's ten terrestrial ecoregions and two marine ecoregions. Coverage is very uneven.

The ecoregions are grouped into Global Ecosystem Typology biomes using the crosswalk in country.json. Rivers come from HydroRIVERS, and lakes and wetlands from GLWD v2 (script 03_wetlands_glwd.py), counting river channels by length only and leaving out rice paddies.

Finding. The Sahara is well covered, almost entirely through OECMs; the Mediterranean north is not. Mediterranean woodlands and forests (135,146 km²) are 13.3% covered and Mediterranean conifer forests 8.5%, with no OECM contribution. Salt pans and chotts are 85% covered, but marshes only 15% and riverine wetlands 20%. The river figure of 53% is mostly dry Saharan wadis inside the Cultural Parks. At sea, both marine ecoregions are under 0.4%.
STEP 08Sheet: KBA_Coverage · script 02_kba_overlay.py

Key Biodiversity Areas

Each of the 65 KBA polygons is overlaid with the flat PA layer and the PA-erased OECM layer, so the sheet shows both sources of coverage. Two KBAs (Béni Abbès, Garaet Haouas) exist only as points with no area and cannot be measured.

Realm rules, as for any country: a KBA is marine with at least 5% of its area outside the land, mountain with at least 5% inside the GMBA mountain inventory, and freshwater if its factsheet, name or trigger species point to fresh water.

Finding. On average, Algerian KBAs are 43.5% covered, 35.1% by protected areas alone. Seventeen are fully covered, mostly Saharan sites and wetlands. Mountain KBAs are the gap: 42 KBAs, average 22.7%, and 24 with no coverage at all. The largest unprotected areas are all in the northern mountains: Aurès–Chélia (4,845 km²), the Dahra range (3,415 km²), Monts des Traras (1,687 km²), Haut Seybouse and the Bibans.

The PA-only results match the KBA database's own coverage figures for every KBA, which confirms the protected-area layer. The difference is the OECMs, which the KBA database doesn't count: Ahaggar rises from 0.8% to 100%, Djebel Amour from 0% to 100%. One Moroccan KBA (Jbel Krouz) overlaps Algerian land by 85 km² along the border; it is listed in kba_foreign_overlapping_DZA.csv and not counted.

STEP 09Sheet: Governance

Governance

Finding. Every reported site is governed by a national ministry, apart from two with no governance type: the Tassili n'Ajjer World Heritage site and Djebel Aïssa National Park. Because Tassili is so large, they make up 6.0% of the covered area, above the 5% QC threshold, so the check raises a warning. A WDPA update for Tassili would clear it.
STEP 10Sheet: Mgmt_Effectiveness

Management effectiveness

Sixteen site pages list GD-PAME evaluations: METT assessments of national parks (2013, 2014) and of the four larger Cultural Parks (2011), BREMi evaluations of the biosphere reserves (2013), BirdLife IBA monitoring (2001) and World Heritage Outlooks for Tassili n'Ajjer up to 2025.

Evaluations on excluded records

Six BREMi evaluations sit on biosphere-reserve records, which the method excludes. They were moved to the national park or World Heritage record covering the same site (Tassili, Chréa, El Kala, Taza, Gouraya, Djurdjura), as listed in country.json. Column Y records each move.

Only Tassili has an evaluation from the last five years, and none has a numeric score. The Cultural Parks, 91% of the covered land, were last assessed in 2011. Report this breakdown as provisional.

STEP 11Sheet: Time_Series

Time series

Almost all of today's land coverage comes from three moments: Ahaggar in 1987, and Tindouf, Touat-Gourara-Tidikelt and Atlas Saharien in 2008. Protected areas alone have grown slowly, from 3.3% in 1983 to 4.65%, mainly through the 2001–2004 Ramsar listings. At sea, the Cap Lindles reserve (2019) doubled a very small figure.

One limit of the net-area method: because protected areas take precedence, the part of the Tassili Cultural Park that overlaps the 1982 World Heritage site is dated 1982, not 1972. The curve therefore understates coverage between 1972 and 1982.

The workbook and Protected Planet agree within 0.1 points. The national report uses the legal areas of the Cultural Parks and the official national territory (2,381,741 km²), so its 43.77% is not directly comparable: the WDPA outlines of the parks are larger (1,233,094 km² before overlaps are removed). Explain this in the report rather than mixing the two sources. The planned Saoura Cultural Park (92,014 km²) would add about 4 points once it is designated and reported.

STEP 13Sheet: Dashboard

Report

In 2026, protected areas covered 107,764 km², or 4.65% of Algeria's terrestrial and inland water area, and other effective area-based conservation measures (the five Cultural Parks) a further 1,151,696 km², or 49.67%; together 54.32% (Headline Indicator 3.1; WDPA/WD-OECM October 2026; Mollweide equal-area). Marine and coastal coverage was 90 km², or 0.07%; reaching 30% would require a further 39,342 km², and the national 5% target a further 6,482 km². On average, Algeria's 65 Key Biodiversity Areas are 43.5% covered (mountain KBAs 22.7%). Coverage ranges from 8.5% of Mediterranean conifer forests to over 99% of Saharan montane woodlands. All reported sites are government-governed; 6% of the covered area has a current but unscored effectiveness evaluation.
STEP 14

What this example leaves out

  1. The OECM evidence. The tool counts the Cultural Parks as reported. Checking each against the CBD 14/8 criteria is a national task, not a GIS step.
  2. Marine biomes. Splitting the sea into shelf and pelagic waters needs bathymetry.
  3. Effectiveness scores. GD-PAME gives the method and year, not the score.
  4. Sites not yet in the WDPA. Recent national parks and the planned Saoura Cultural Park are missing until reported to UNEP-WCMC.
