GBF Headline Indicator 3.1 · National worked example with real data

Libya, step by step through the 30×30 tool

This guide fills the Indicator 3.1 Excel tool for Libya from the 5 records in the October 2026 World Database on Protected Areas, the 18 official Key Biodiversity Area polygons (September 2026), and a GIS run over land and sea. Every number comes from those data and from the workbook recalculated in Excel.

Libya is the opposite of the large-OECM cases. The WDPA holds only five sites, all as points with a reported area, and no OECMs. The tool still works, but every figure rests on circles drawn around five coordinates, and the main finding is a data gap: the reserves Libya has declared since 2010 have not reached the global database.

STEP 01

Collect the data

  1. Protected areas. WDPA, October 2026, from the UNEP-WCMC map service behind Protected Planet: five point records and no polygons. The WD-OECM has no Libyan records.
  2. Base map. Land: the geoBoundaries ADM0 outline. Sea: the Marine Regions EEZ v12 "Libyan Exclusive Economic Zone", as used by Protected Planet; the 8,114 km² zone claimed by both Egypt and Libya is kept for reference only. Coastal gaps (176 km²) are closed.
  3. Key Biodiversity Areas. The World Database of KBAs, September 2026: 18 Libyan polygons and 51 trigger-species records. Licensed data; don't republish the polygons.
  4. Management effectiveness. No GD-PAME evaluation is listed for any of the five sites.
  5. Ecosystems and inland waters. RESOLVE Ecoregions 2017, Marine Ecoregions of the World, GMBA v2.0, HydroRIVERS and GLWD v2, clipped by 00_prepare_layers.py.
  6. National documents. Libya's National Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan 2023–2030. No CBD national report could be retrieved.

STEP 03Outside Excel: 03_analysis/scripts/01_gis_flatten.py

The points-only problem

When a site has no boundary, the metadata says to draw a circle with the reported area around the reported point. For Libya that is the whole method: the five circles are the protected-area layer. They don't overlap, so flattening changes nothing.

Why this matters

A circle can't follow a coastline or a valley. El Nagaza National Park is reported as 4,000 km² and as marine; its circle, 35.7 km in radius around 32.67°N 14.28°E, falls 1,759 km² on land and 2,241 km² at sea. That single site is 84% of the land figure and all of the sea figure. A real boundary for El Nagaza, or for Wadi El Kouf in the Jabal al Akhdar, would change the result more than any other step in this guide.

The register flags all five sites "Buffered point – coverage approximate". The fix is a data fix: send UNEP-WCMC the boundaries.

STEP 05Sheet: Headline_3.1

Read the headline

Result. Protected areas cover 0.13% of Libya's land (2,099 km²) and 0.63% of its waters (2,241 km²). Reaching 30% would take another 486,952 km² on land and 104,956 km² at sea. Libya's own target 3 in its NBSAP is the same 30% by 2030.
STEP 06Sheet: Ecosystems

Ecosystems and inland waters

Finding. Seven of Libya's nine land ecoregions, and 96% of its land, have no protection at all; the deserts (biome T5, 1.57 million km²) are at 0%. The two Mediterranean ecoregions of the coast reach 2.5% and 3.4%. Of the 4,997 km² of salt pans and sebkhas, the country's main wetland type, none lies in a protected area; all lakes and wetlands together are 0.3% covered.
STEP 07Sheet: KBA_Coverage · script 02_kba_overlay.py

Key Biodiversity Areas

Finding. Sixteen of Libya's 18 KBAs have no protection. The two that do are covered only by circles: Jabal al Akhdar (11,106 km²) by the Wadi El Kouf circle, 2.9%; and Nefhusa (13,421 km²) by the land half of the El Nagaza circle, 2.8%. The seven marine KBAs, including the Gulf of Sirte, Bumbah Gulf and Farwa Island, are at 0%.

The KBA database itself reports 0% for every Libyan KBA, because it doesn't buffer point-only protected areas. That is the only difference between the two sources. Two foreign KBAs overlap Libyan land along the borders: Tassili n'Ajjer in Algeria (552 km²) and Sallum in Egypt (39 km²); they are listed in kba_foreign_overlapping_LBY.csv and not counted.

STEP 08Sheets: Governance, Mgmt_Effectiveness

Governance and effectiveness

All five sites are governed by the national government, and none has a recorded management-effectiveness evaluation, so 100% of the covered area is "Not assessed". The two Ramsar records state that their management plans are "not implemented and not available".

STEP 09Sheet: Time_Series

Time series

Coverage was set by three designations: Bier Ayad (1976), Wadi El Kouf (1978) and El Nagaza (1992); the two Ramsar springs (2000) add under 1 km². Nothing designated since 2000 appears in the WDPA.

The workbook matches Protected Planet. The NBSAP describes a larger network: 11 reserves by 2010 and 30 new sites announced in 2021, none of which is in the WDPA. Until they are reported to UNEP-WCMC with boundaries, they can't enter the global indicator. Submitting them is the single most useful step Libya can take for Target 3 reporting.

STEP 11Sheet: Dashboard

Report

In 2026, the protected areas reported in the WDPA covered 2,099 km², or 0.13% of Libya's terrestrial and inland water area, and 2,241 km², or 0.63% of its marine and coastal area (Headline Indicator 3.1; WDPA October 2026; Mollweide equal-area). All five sites are recorded as points, so these areas are approximations; no OECMs have been reported. Reaching 30% would require a further 486,952 km² on land and 104,956 km² at sea. Sixteen of Libya's 18 Key Biodiversity Areas have no protection. Reserves declared nationally since 2010, including 22 announced in 2021, are not yet included in the WDPA.
STEP 12

What this example leaves out

  1. Libya's national network. The reserves, wetlands and wildlife sites announced in 2021 are not in the WDPA; with their boundaries the whole workbook would change.
  2. Real boundaries for the five sites. The circles are a stand-in required by the method.
  3. OECMs. None reported; candidates could include traditionally managed oases and grazing reserves, if they meet the CBD 14/8 criteria.
  4. Marine biomes and effectiveness scores. Bathymetry and assessment data are not available here.
