GBF Headline Indicator 3.1 · Worked example with real data

Andorra, step by step through the 30×30 tool

This guide fills the Indicator 3.1 Excel tool for Andorra, using the country's 11 protected-area records from the October 2026 World Database on Protected Areas. Every number below comes from those records, a real GIS run, and the workbook recalculated in Excel.

Andorra is a good first case. It is landlocked, so only the terrestrial realm applies. It has 11 sites and no OECMs. Its sites also overlap heavily, which shows why flattening matters, and it sits just below the 30% target.

STEP 01

Collect the data

The tool needs four inputs. For Andorra they came from these sources:

  1. Protected areas and OECMs. The WDPA and WD-OECM, October 2026 release, from Protected Planet. Andorra has 11 WDPA polygons and no OECM records. The October 2026 schema uses SITE_ID and REALM where older releases used WDPAID and MARINE.
  2. A national boundary for the denominator. Here the open geoBoundaries ADM0 outline, projected to Mollweide: 470.45 km². Use your official national boundary if you have one, and use the same one for every area in the workbook.
  3. Key Biodiversity Areas. From the World Database of KBAs. Andorra has one KBA, Pirineo de Andorra (SitRecID 19).
  4. Management-effectiveness records. From the Global Database on Protected Area Management Effectiveness (GD-PAME), shown on each site's Protected Planet page.

Download the country's polygons and points as a shapefile or GeoPackage. For a scripted workflow, the R package wdpar or BIOPAMA's protection_analyst (Python) will fetch and clean them for you.

STEP 02Sheet: Setup

Fill in Setup

Setup holds everything the formulas divide by, plus your declarations about how the GIS work was done. Fill only the yellow cells. Andorra has no sea, so the marine area is 0. The tool then marks the marine row "Not applicable" instead of raising a warning.

Input you type or pasteFormula, computed by the toolKey resultFlag to act on

Rows 14–17 aren't decoration. Each one feeds a check on the QC sheet, so you can't claim a flattened, equal-area result without saying so. Row 26 sets how old an effectiveness assessment can be and still count as current; five years is a national choice, not part of the official metadata.

STEP 03Outside Excel: QGIS, R or Python

Flatten the overlaps in GIS

This is the step Excel can't do, and the one that matters most here. Several Andorran parks appear two, three or four times in the WDPA: as a municipal natural park, a Ramsar wetland, an Emerald Network site and a hunting reserve. Summed naively, they give 189 km². The metadata method dissolves all protected-area polygons into one flat layer before measuring, so each square kilometre counts once.

The procedure used here (metadata steps 1–6)

  1. Filter out sites whose status is Proposed or Not Reported, and UNESCO biosphere reserves. None of Andorra's 11 sites is removed.
  2. Buffer point sites to circles of their reported area. Andorra has no points.
  3. Clip to the national boundary and project to Mollweide (ESRI:54009).
  4. Dissolve all polygons. The result is 125.68 km².
  5. To keep per-site figures for the register, give each overlap to the earliest-designated site. The 1993 Sorteny municipal park keeps its area; the 2000 hunting reserve and the 2012 Ramsar record on the same ground get only what is left. The net areas add up exactly to the dissolved total.
STEP 04Sheet: PA_OECM_Register

Enter the site register

Paste one row per WDPA record into the yellow columns, then type the net area from the GIS step into column R. The tool decides inclusion (column Z), picks the area to count (AF) and flags problems (AL) without further input.

Two kinds of flags appear. Three Ramsar records have GOV_TYPE = Not Reported, and the two Emerald Network records have no designation year. Neither stops the calculation, but both affect the breakdowns below.

STEP 05Sheet: Headline_3.1

Read the headline

Indicator 3.1 is the PA area plus the OECM area, overlaps removed, divided by the realm area. For Andorra: 125.68 ÷ 470.45 = 26.7%.

Result. Andorra covers 26.7% of its land with protected areas and needs another 15.5 km² (3.3 percentage points) to reach 30% by 2030. OECMs could close part of that gap; none are reported yet.
STEP 06Sheet: KBA_Coverage

Key Biodiversity Areas

The metadata disaggregation is the mean percentage of each KBA covered by protected areas or OECMs. Andorra's single KBA, Pirineo de Andorra, spans 474.63 km², which is effectively the whole country. The KBA database reports it as 26.20% covered, or 124.35 km². It lies between 838 and 2,942 m, so it counts as terrestrial (SDG 15.1.2) and mountain (SDG 15.4.1). With no ocean overlap, it isn't marine.

With one KBA, the mean equals that site's value: 26.2%. In larger countries the tool averages across hundreds of rows and counts how many KBAs are fully covered (at least 98% by default), partly covered or not covered.

STEP 07Sheet: Governance

Governance

Coverage by governance type is the metadata's proxy for "equitably governed". The tool groups WDPA governance types into the four IUCN types.

Finding. Two-thirds of Andorra's covered area is government-governed. The other third, 41 km² and almost all of it Vall de Madriu-Perafita-Claror, has no governance type reported. The QC sheet raises a warning because more than 5% of the area is unreported. The fix is a data update to the WDPA, not a change to the tool.
STEP 08Sheet: Mgmt_Effectiveness

Management effectiveness

Protected Planet lists GD-PAME evaluations for the Sorteny and Comapedrosa parks: national inventories from 2016 and 2017, covering 26 km².

Pitfall: attributes stranded on records that lost their area

Those evaluations are attached to the Ramsar records. Because the overlap went to the earlier municipal-park records in Step 3, the Ramsar records keep almost no area (0.22 km² and 0 km²). Left as is, the tool would report 0.2 km² assessed instead of 26.

The rule: when overlapping records describe the same physical park, transfer site-level attributes (effectiveness, governance) to the record that holds the area. Here the 2016 and 2017 evaluations were copied onto the two municipal-park rows, with a note in column Y. The assessed area becomes 25.9 km², matching Protected Planet. The tool's ReadMe now includes this rule.

All 25.9 km² are classed Assessment outdated, because 2016 and 2017 are more than five years before 2026. The official methodology for this breakdown is still being developed, so report it as provisional and state the validity window you chose.

STEP 09Sheet: Time_Series

Time series

The tool sums each site's net area cumulatively by designation year. Two Emerald sites have no year. The metadata assigns such sites a random year from the country's other sites, 1,000 times, and takes the median. The tool uses the expected value of that procedure instead. The dashed line shows the lower bound that counts dated sites only.

The 2000 hunting reserves and the 2013 Madriu-Perafita-Claror Ramsar site account for most of the growth. The two undated Emerald sites add only 0.26 km² of new ground, so imputation barely moves the curve here.

STEP 10Sheet: QC_Checks

Quality checks and reconciliation

Both warnings are data gaps already discussed: missing governance types and missing designation years. Next, compare the result with the official figure.

The 0.5-point gap with Protected Planet comes from the base map. Its land area is 6.5 km² smaller than the geoBoundaries outline, and the outlines clip the border parks slightly differently. That is the size of difference the metadata warns about. To match the official figure exactly, use UNEP-WCMC's base map, or type their totals into Setup rows 18–21 and let QC report the difference. The national report figure is higher still, most likely because it uses national definitions and data rather than the WDPA. Explain such gaps in your report rather than mixing sources.

STEP 11Sheet: Dashboard

Report

The Dashboard gathers the figures for the national report. A draft paragraph built only from the workbook might read:

In 2026, protected areas covered 125.7 km², or 26.7% of Andorra's terrestrial and inland water area (Headline Indicator 3.1; WDPA October 2026; Mollweide equal-area). No OECMs have been reported. Reaching the 30% element of Target 3 requires a further 15.5 km². The country's Key Biodiversity Area is 26.2% covered. Of the covered area, 67% is government-governed and 33% has no reported governance type; 21% has a management-effectiveness evaluation, all older than five years.
STEP 12

What this example leaves out

Two parts of the workbook stay empty in the Andorra file, by design:

  1. Ecosystems. The biome and ecoregion breakdown needs a national ecosystem map cross-walked to the IUCN Global Ecosystem Typology, intersected with the flat layer in GIS. No such map was available for this example, so the sheet is left empty rather than filled with guesses.
  2. Inland waters. River length and lake and wetland area covered need RiverATLAS and GLWD v2, or national hydrography, overlaid in GIS. Not computed here.

Both follow the same pattern as the KBA step: compute the extent and the overlap in GIS, then type the numbers into the yellow cells.
